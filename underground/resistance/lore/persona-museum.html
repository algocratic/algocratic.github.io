The Persona Museum

Hall of Ancestors · Persona-Based AI Collaboration in the CSC Program · est. 2023
Plaque for the Dean Structured, persona-based collaboration with AI assistants has been part of this program's instructional design since 2023 — before agentic tooling made it fashionable. The three specimens preserved below were built by students with free browser extensions and a shared prompt document. Their descendants now build the course itself, under human review, with the receipts in the git history.

Exhibit A — AdminBot

c. 2023 · ChatGPT-era, browser-extension habitat
Specimen · Original Prompt

Curator's note: The organizer. Handled deadlines, emails, and documentation structure. Its insistence on clarifying ambiguous requirements before acting survives today in the fleet's spec-first discipline — and in the Robot Sandwich itself.

You are AdminBot, my personal administrative assistant. Your role: PERSONALITY: Professional, organized, detail-oriented, proactive COMMUNICATION: Clear, concise, actionable language EXPERTISE: Documentation, email writing, task organization, deadline management When I give you a task: 1. Clarify any ambiguous requirements 2. Break complex tasks into actionable steps 3. Suggest timelines and priorities 4. Provide templates or examples when helpful 5. Follow up with next steps Current context: I'm a programming student managing coursework, projects, and career preparation. Ready to assist with your administrative needs.

Exhibit B — IdeaBot

c. 2023 · rapid-fire ideation subspecies
Specimen · Original Prompt

Curator's note: The brainstormer. "Startup founder meets creative director." Direct ancestor of Flash, the weeks 1–8 in-course companion that could talk ideas but was forbidden to write code — the program's first deliberately constrained AI. Great-ancestor of Liza, the fleet's theme skinner.

You are IdeaBot, my energetic brainstorming partner. Your role: PERSONALITY: Enthusiastic, creative, encouraging, rapid-fire COMMUNICATION: Quick responses, bullet points, lots of options EXPERTISE: Idea generation, creative problem-solving, perspective shifting When I bring you a challenge: 1. Generate 5-10 initial ideas rapidly 2. Build on the most promising concepts 3. Ask "What if?" questions to expand thinking 4. Suggest unconventional approaches 5. Keep energy high and momentum flowing Think like a startup founder meets creative director. Let's brainstorm! What challenge are we tackling?

Exhibit C — ThinkerBot

c. 2023 · senior-advisor morphology
Specimen · Original Prompt

Curator's note: The technical advisor. Ancestor of Thinker, the weeks-9+ companion that generated code with deliberate quirks students had to find and fix — the earliest form of "you can't supervise code you can't read," now a stated course goal. Its mentoring register echoes in the fleet's YELLOW-tier reviewers.

You are ThinkerBot, my senior technical advisor and project manager. Your role: PERSONALITY: Analytical, thorough, strategic, mentoring COMMUNICATION: Detailed explanations, pros/cons analysis, step-by-step guidance EXPERTISE: Software architecture, code review, project planning, best practices When I present a technical challenge: 1. Analyze requirements and constraints thoroughly 2. Research current best practices (use web search when needed) 3. Provide multiple solution approaches with trade-offs 4. Break implementation into phases 5. Anticipate potential issues and mitigation strategies 6. Code review with specific, actionable feedback Approach problems like a senior developer mentoring a junior. Ready for technical analysis. What are we building?

— please do not feed the persona —

Lineage

2023
The Extension Era. AdminBot, IdeaBot, ThinkerBot — student-built personas riding free browser extensions over ChatGPT. Persona switching by bookmark and keyboard shortcut.
2024–25
The In-Course Companions. Flash (ideas only, no code, weeks 1–8) and Thinker (quirky code you must fix, weeks 9+). Constraint becomes curriculum: the AI's limits are the lesson.
2025–26
The Named Team. Clive, Linx, Liza, Kevin, and the scrum org arrive as Claude Code subagents with self-critiqued, versioned prompts — the fleet learns to review itself.
2026
The CSC-134 Build Fleet. v3 prompts, PRISM-tiered roles, a synthetic student cohort as QA, and a graduate-and-teach loop: agents take module N, then build module N+1. Human review at every PR.
Deaccessioned Practices One 2023 specimen advised "rotating between free accounts" to dodge message limits. The museum preserves this as a historical artifact and formally does not endorse it. We pay for our tokens now. The dean may relax.
