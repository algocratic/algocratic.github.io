PRISM cross-reference · audience alignment

PRISM × DESIGN SYSTEM

A semantic contract for matching conceptual decision space to the people expected to navigate it. Color indicates capability, ownership, and audience complexity; it is not ornament.

Cross-reference finding

The canonical PRISM framework and the Algocratic Futures Design System agree on one ladder with two measurement lenses. Below GREEN, progression describes increasingly complex making. Above GREEN, progression describes increasingly broad coordination and organizational ownership. GREEN is the hinge: an independently shipped and operated product.

Warm bands · makers

INFRARED through YELLOW use Bloom’s taxonomy to describe cognitive complexity: remember, understand, apply, analyze, and evaluate. The audience moves from guided micro-tasks to ambiguous feature work, architecture, debugging, and mentorship.

Cool bands · orchestrators

BLUE through ULTRAVIOLET use scope of ownership: process, project, program, organization, and field. They do not claim to extend Bloom’s taxonomy beyond “create.” The joys of becoming managerial class are sometimes declined.

GREEN · Bloom: create · owns a deployed product end-to-end · job-ready ship-it pivot

For portal authors, “clearance” therefore names the complexity and scope a reader is being asked to hold. It supports orientation and progressive disclosure; it does not withhold public content or imply institutional rank.

Exact semantic design rules

  1. PRISM colors are semantic, never decorative. Use a band only when the element communicates that band’s competency, clearance, ownership, or audience-complexity meaning.
  2. Preserve the lens change. INFRARED–YELLOW are graded by Bloom’s cognitive lens; GREEN is “create” and independent delivery; BLUE–ULTRAVIOLET are graded by ownership scope.
  3. Preserve the anatomy. A ladder rung contains band and role, scope, lens/course tags, and the evidence bank (“Module Zero +”). Place the GREEN hinge between BLUE and GREEN.
  4. Mark the invisible ends. INFRARED and ULTRAVIOLET use dashed borders. An open or hovered rung may receive a gold left spine.
  5. Order the ladder by increasing ownership. In the canonical vertical display, ULTRAVIOLET is at the top and INFRARED at the bottom. A spectrum rail may run infrared → ultraviolet when its directional label makes that reading explicit.
  6. Bank evidence. Each level names observable artifacts, not personality traits. Module Zero recurs across courses, so evidence accumulates in a living competency record.
  7. Design for the audience; do not gate the audience. Higher-complexity pages receive summaries, prerequisites, definitions, source trails, and progressive disclosure. Clearance is an orientation device, not access control.
  8. Keep GRAY outside the spectrum. GRAY (#53565A) means Gather Required Aesthetic Yields, the collaboration band; it is not a tenth PRISM clearance rung.
  9. Use the document family for PRISM editorial work. Source Serif 4 and Source Sans 3 form the PRISM house style. Michroma is a substitute display face, ALL CAPS only and never body copy. JetBrains Mono carries stamps, sources, IDs, and counts.
  10. Keep surfaces disciplined. Paper documents use white / #FAF9F6, navy headings, silver hairlines, and one sparse gold glint. Cards are flat, lightly rounded, and nearly shadowless; motion is restrained.
  11. Use real marks. Use supplied brand assets rather than drawn replacements. Do not use emoji. Status uses dots, counts, or mono strings; utility icons, if introduced, must be flagged as an extension.
  12. Make rigor carry the joke. The institutional voice may be deadpan and dystopian, but claims, receipts, limitations, and accessibility remain sincere. “INCONCLUSIVE” is not “PASS.”

Portal application decision

Every revised portal page should declare or visibly imply its intended PRISM audience, lead with the smallest useful summary, and disclose the next layer of complexity with headings, links, or native disclosure controls. Band color may reinforce that declaration, but typography, labeling, and structure must communicate the same meaning without color.

This aligns conceptual decision space with real-world audience complexity while keeping locally reviewed pages public and directly inspectable. The crosswalk describes presentation and information architecture; it does not confer credentials, restrict files, or claim that one kind of work is intrinsically superior to another.
